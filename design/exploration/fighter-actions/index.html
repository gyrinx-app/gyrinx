Earlier design questions

The fighter card shows the model’s current characteristics and equipment tiers. A separate card on the fighter edit view shows available improvements, costs and unfinished choices. Both examples use this layout.

Spending a counter

A Spyrer has 8 Kill Count. Taking one Suit Evolution spends 4 and leaves 4. The remaining four allow another use.

Reaching an XP rank

A fighter recruited at 37 XP reaches 49 XP. They gain one advancement and keep 49 XP. We record that the advancement for 49 XP has been taken. The next is at 61 XP.

1. Where should we list these improvements?

Option A: use a collection. Show the improvement, its requirements and any payment in the familiar catalogue. Adding it gives the player a choice to complete.

Option B: put payment and eligibility on each slot. This keeps the rules beside the choice, but duplicates behaviour that purchases already need.

Recommendation: use collections for the catalogue. Keep the requirement separate from the price. An XP advancement has a requirement and no payment; Suit Evolution has a four-kill payment.

Work required: collections currently price credits and Trade Points. They cannot directly contain a Slot or Pickable. We need to decide how a collection entry gives a choice, and add counter payments.

2. What prevents the same advancement being taken twice?

Checking “XP is at least 49” is insufficient because XP stays at 49 after advancing. Record which rank each advancement belongs to.

If the fighter moves from 48 to 61 XP, offer two separate advancements: one for 49 and one for 61. Starting XP must not create advancements for earlier ranks.

Recommendation: create one saved choice per newly reached rank. A paid action can save the same kind of choice before checkout. The screens that complete those choices can then be shared.

Trade-off: this needs records for earned and completed choices, not just a threshold field. In return, it handles multiple ranks, repeated purchases and unfinished choices without confusing them.

3. Payment timing — agreed

Choose the outcome, item and tier before paying. The final confirmation writes the payment and result together. Saved unfinished choices remain unpaid and can be cancelled without a refund.

The prototype and data model now follow this order. XP advancements reserve their earned allowance before rolling; XP remains unchanged.

4. How should the next choice appear?

Some results need another selection. A skill result needs a skill. An augmentation needs an item and a tier.

Option A: share the continuation screen. After saving, show any unfinished choice that the result created. The content specifies what to choose; the screen does not contain a list of Spyrer steps.

Option B: show a link to the existing edit page. This takes less work, but the player must find the right item and remember to finish.

Recommendation: use the shared continuation screen. Ordinary choices already fit slots and picks. Updating one carried item's existing tier still needs new behaviour: selecting the item, changing its slot, and recording that the original choice is complete.

5. Correcting a completed result — agreed

Keep the original ActionRecord, payment, earned allowance and recorded roll. Correct the selected result and record what changed. An augmentation can move to another carried item when the original change can be safely reversed. Reverse the old result and apply the replacement together.

If a later upgrade or other dependent change prevents safe reversal, show the conflicting change and require it to be resolved first. Editing or removing a result never refunds automatically. An explicit Undo and refund would reverse the result and original payment together; including that operation in the first release remains undecided.

This policy is recorded in the data proposal. Correction controls are not yet part of this interactive prototype.

6. Which additional rules should we include in the first implementation?

Hunt Master receives one item augmentation on recruitment. Suit Maintenance spends 100 credits to clear glitches. Both could use the same choices and operations.

Recommendation: include the free Hunt Master choice to check that the item picker works without a payment. Add Suit Maintenance after the clear-glitches operation works.

Moving a Spyrer weapon to the stash removes its augmentations. The equipment transfer operation must follow that rule. It cannot preserve tiers in the stash.

Clear glitches must reset Glitch Count and remove every glitch pick. It should happen as one operation so the count and penalties cannot disagree.
