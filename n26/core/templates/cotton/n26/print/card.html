{% comment %} — one thing that arrives on paper whole. {# fills its grid cell, as tall as its content #} … {# a fixed physical size, with a footer pinned to the bottom edge #} … Width comes from the grid's cell by default, so a card tiled two-up and the same card tiled three-up are the same call. Standing alone, outside a grid, it fills its container instead — the custom property simply is not there and the fallback takes over. Height is the interesting prop. Left off, the card is as tall as its content and a row of them is as tall as the tallest. Given a length, the card becomes a fixed physical object — the shape you want when it is going to be cut out — and then the body takes the slack and the `foot` slot sits on the bottom edge however little there is above it. Content past the bottom is clipped rather than spilling onto the next card; `data-print-fit` and are how you avoid needing to. A card cannot be split by the page fold. Not because of the break-inside rules it carries — those are belt and braces for engines that ignore the model — but because it is an atomic inline. See for why that is the whole design. title, subtitle, badge — the head band: a name, a line under it, and a number opposite. All optional; give none and no band is drawn, and the slot owns the whole card. width, height — CSS lengths. Width defaults to the grid cell. pad — inner padding. fit — mark the body for the shrink-to-fit script, so content too long for a fixed-height card reduces its type instead of being cut off. Needs to have loaded the script, and means nothing without a height — an auto-height card has nothing to overflow. fit_min — the floor it shrinks to, in millimetres. Below that it clips again, because type too small to read is not a rescue. {% endcomment %}
