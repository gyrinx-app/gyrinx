{% comment %} — how a form ends: the way out, then the act. Every form on the site ends the same way, and this is where that is decided once. draws its footer with this, so a full-page form gets it by filling in props; anything that is not a form-page — a dialog, or one of several forms on a page — writes the tag itself. Two hosts, one definition, and no screen composing a footer of its own. --- the order and the alignment are not props --- The way out sits first, the act last, and the row is right-aligned where the eye finishes the last field. The act comes last because it is what the form is for, and a reader running down the fields meets every alternative — the way out, then any lesser act — on the way to it. Neither is a choice a call site can make: buttons that swap places between screens are buttons a reader has to read before clicking. --- cancel is a link, and it is quiet --- Cancel navigates. It is an href — never a submit, and never a scripted step back through history — because leaving is not a submission, and a reader who clicks it should land where they already were rather than wherever this form would have sent them. It is drawn only when it has somewhere to lead: a form with nowhere to go back to leaves cancel_url out and gets no cancel at all, which is better than one that goes somewhere arbitrary. Ghost, so the two do not compete. The act carries the colour that says what it does — green brings something into existence, red takes it away — and a cancel of equal weight beside it makes a reader stop and read both to find the one they want. Text at rest and a button under the pointer is the right weight for a control that is only wanted when the answer is no. --- a form with two acts --- Only the submit that was clicked is sent, which is how a form offering two acts tells them apart: the second one goes in the slot carrying its own name and value, and the act at the end needs no name, because its click is the one that sends nothing extra. submit_label — the act's wording. Without one no submit is drawn. submit_variant — the act's colour. `success` by default, because the button that ends a form usually brings a thing into existence; `danger` where it takes one away, `primary` where it only carries on. cancel_url — where the way out leads. Without one there is no cancel. cancel_label — the way out's wording. "Cancel" by default. size — both buttons' size, for a footer that ends a step rather than a screen. slot — further controls, drawn between the way out and the act. For the rare form answering in more than one way: the reader still meets Cancel first and the form's own act still ends the row. {% endcomment %} {% comment %} Wrapping rather than shrinking: on a narrow screen two buttons that will not fit on one line drop onto two, still right-aligned, rather than squeezing their wording to nothing. {% endcomment %}
