Two controls joined into one: a link that says what you are on and goes to it, and a chevron that opens the list of everything you could be on instead. The pair reads as a single object — this thing, and the others like it — which is what a switcher is. A breadcrumb tells you where you are; this tells you where you are and offers the alternatives in the same gesture. --- the link is optional, and that is the point --- Drop `label` and only the chevron renders. Some places already name the current thing a line above, in a heading a switcher would only repeat; others sit in a row of controls where the name has nowhere to go. So the identifier is a variant, not a fixture, and the chevron alone is a whole control: with one child, the button group rounds both its ends rather than leaving a shape cut in half. --- with no script --- The panel is the kit's dropdown, which Alpine reveals; until it runs, the panel is hidden.
