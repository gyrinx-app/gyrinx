A ghost button group: a button holding the name of the thing you are on and linking to it, joined to a chevron button that opens the list of everything you could be on instead. The pair reads as a single object — this thing, and the others like it — which is what a switcher is. A breadcrumb tells you where you are; this tells you where you are and offers the alternatives in the same gesture. --- quiet at rest, a button under the pointer --- Both halves take the text colour of whatever they sit in and carry no fill and no rule, so the control reads as words in a bar or beside a heading rather than as furniture competing with the page's own name. The hover fill is the whole of the affordance, and it is per half: hover the name and it is the way to the thing, hover the chevron and it is the way to the rest. The chevron also turns over while the panel is open, and says so through aria-expanded. They are as narrow as they can be while staying clickable. The padding is cut well below what a button this size asks for, so nothing sits between the two halves and the pair sits close to whatever it was put beside. --- two shapes, one component --- Drop `label` and only the chevron renders. Some places already name the current thing a line above, in a heading a switcher would only repeat; others sit in a row of controls where the name has nowhere to go. So the identifier is a variant, not a fixture, and the chevron alone is a whole control: with one child, the button group rounds both its ends rather than leaving a shape cut in half. Every placement — the top bar, a page's heading, an authoring header — calls this component and picks its shape by passing a label or not. Anything a placement needs that this cannot say is a prop to add here, not a reason to write the markup again somewhere else. --- with no script --- The panel is the kit's dropdown, which Alpine reveals; until it runs, the panel is hidden.
