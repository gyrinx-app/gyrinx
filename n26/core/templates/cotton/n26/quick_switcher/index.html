{% comment %} — the thing you are looking at, and a short way to its siblings. Two things side by side: a link that says what you are on and goes to it, and a chevron that opens the list of everything you could be on instead. The pair reads as a single object — this thing, and the others like it — which is what a switcher is. A breadcrumb tells you where you are; this tells you where you are and offers the alternatives in the same gesture. --- two controls that must not look like one --- They do different things, so they are drawn as different kinds of thing. The name is the house inline link — accent, underlined on hover — because it is a link, to the thing it names; drawn as a quiet button it reads as a label, and a reader who presses it expecting the list gets a page instead. The chevron is a button with the app's spelling of "this opens more": it turns over while the panel is open, and says so through aria-expanded. Both are as narrow as they can be. The name carries no padding at all and the chevron only what a pressable square needs, so the two sit against each other and against whatever the switcher was put beside — a heading, or a page's name in the top bar. Space between them would read as two controls that happen to be near each other. --- the link is optional, and that is the point --- Drop `label` and only the chevron renders. Some places already name the current thing a line above, in a heading a switcher would only repeat; others sit in a row of controls where the name has nowhere to go. So the identifier is a variant, not a fixture, and the chevron alone is a whole control. --- with no script --- The panel is the kit's dropdown, which Alpine reveals; until it runs, the panel is hidden.
