{% comment %} — a fighter's card: who they are, what they can do, what they carry. Takes a n26.render.ModelCard, which core/render.py already assembles in one query. Nothing here computes anything: values arrive formatted, weapon costs are summed by WeaponLine, and where a statline breaks into groups is content. The template's whole job is layout. It follows the rulebook's Model Card anatomy — name, cost, type, characteristics, weapons, skills, equipment, current/target XP — and then keeps going where being digital allows. The paper card cannot say *why* a characteristic differs from the printed one, that a trait was granted rather than printed, that a gun has paid ammo hung off it, or that a choice is still open. All four are already in the data. Read-only about the *fighter*: nothing here changes a statline, a weapon or a skill, and there are no controls over any of them. Notes and lore are the two exceptions and they are not really exceptions — they are the only things on a card a player writes rather than earns, and an Edit beside each is how you find out you can. Everything else is still a rendering. Two composition notes. Every region is a kit component or one of ours except the tables, which are composed from theme tokens — see for why c-ui.table could not be reused. And almost nothing is greyed out: hierarchy comes from weight and size, and an empty value is an em dash rather than a muted "None", so a card has no second-class text on it. Two exceptions, both captions rather than content — the profile name under the model's, and the XP target beside the current one. Neither is a value a player looks up; each labels the thing next to it, which is the only thing grey is for here. --- what it deliberately leaves out --- `card.collections` (which lists a fighter can shop at) and `card.effects` (what their kit does beyond this card) are both on the structure and neither is drawn (Tom, 2026-08-07). They are reference rather than identity: true of a fighter, and not what anyone is reading a card to find out. On a gang sheet of eleven cards they were two more rows between the statline and the weapons on every one of them. Buying is reached from Equip in the `actions` slot, which is the answer to the question "Buys from" was there to answer, and closer to hand. :card — a n26.render.ModelCard. avatar — a picture of the model, before the name. Any markup; it is framed and clipped here. An fills the frame; gives the circular treatment and an initials fallback instead. actions — what can be done to this fighter, on the header line between the name and the rating. A slot, so a preview card can have none: the hire list draws one of these behind every row and nothing on it should be pressable. notes — the player's own notes about this fighter, as rendered markup. lore — their backstory, the same. notes_href, lore_href — where each Edit goes. Either alone is enough to draw the section: an empty one with an Edit is how a reader learns they can write it. Both are props rather than card.notes and card.lore because n26.render.ModelCard has neither field yet — see design/asks/model-card-notes- lore.md. When it grows them, call sites pass card.notes and this stays as is. choose_href — where an unresolved choice sends a reader. Left empty the prompt still reads as a sentence, because c-n26.link renders a span with no href — which is what a print sheet and a preview want. {% endcomment %} {% comment %} One line: the name, what you can do, what it costs. The name takes the room that is going and wraps inside it; everything to its right is shrink-0 and holds its place. That is the whole reason this is one row rather than a name above a run of buttons — a fighter called The Silent Ledger and the Long Count should cost the card a second line of *name*, not a second row of chrome, and the controls should not move down the card because someone had a long name. No truncation. The name is the one thing on a card that cannot be worked out from anything else on it, which is the same call the profile picker's rows make. items-start, so the buttons and the rating stay level with the first line of a wrapping name instead of drifting to its middle. {% endcomment %} {% comment %} -mx-2 because the kit's header band is px-6 and hardcodes it, where every row below is px-4: the name started eight pixels further in than Skills and the rating ended eight pixels further out, so the widest line on the card had the least room to be wide in. Pulling back two is what puts the header on the same margin as the tables. A negative margin rather than a rule of our own, because the padding is a utility on a kit element we do not own — there is no prop for it, and anything overriding it would have to be unlayered and keyed on a class added for the purpose. Two pixels of arithmetic against one hardcoded value is the smaller thing to keep in step. {% endcomment %}
