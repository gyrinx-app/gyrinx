Drawn the way a card draws the same rows, so a reader recognises what they are looking at: the thing, what it contributed, and its parts indented under it. The weapon's own firing line is not among them, for the same reason the card gives it no row of its own — it *is* the weapon. --- what each line can do --- Sell and the menu, in that order, because selling is the one anybody came here to do; handing a thing to another fighter and taking it off the roster are the rarer two and share a chevron. Sell is red: it takes a thing away, whatever it puts in the bank. A part offers no move. It belongs to the thing it hangs off — ammo is ammo *for* a gun — and Operation.move refuses an assignment with a parent, so offering it here would be offering a press that cannot work. Every control is a link to a real address: the dialog is a server state, so the press that opens it is a navigation and not a script. That also keeps this out of the listing's own form, which wraps every row on the page — a form inside a form is not a thing HTML has. :things — a list of n26.core.owned.OwnedThing, links already filled in. {% endcomment %}
