{% comment %} — what the model is already carrying, and what can happen to it. Takes n26.core.listing.OwnedCopyRow: one line per copy the model holds, with a line beneath for each part hanging off it — a gun's paid ammo, a sight bolted to it. Drawn the way a card draws the same rows, so a reader recognises what they are looking at: the thing, what it contributed, and its parts indented under it. The weapon's own firing line is not among them, for the same reason the card gives it no row of its own — it *is* the weapon. --- what each line can do --- Each copy carries its own acts, and this draws them in the order the structure gives: the one act in the open, then the rest behind a chevron. Selling is the one anybody came here to do; handing a thing to another fighter and taking it off the roster are the rarer ones. Which of them is red is the row's word and not this template's — see n26/includes/row_action.html — so an act added to the structure appears here in the right colour with nothing edited. A part offers no move. It belongs to the thing it hangs off — ammo is ammo *for* a gun — and Operation.move refuses a firing line, so the structure gives it none to draw. An accessory is the case that could be moved, but the way to re-fit one is from wherever it is being kept rather than from the gun it is already on. A weapon carries one extra control: the way to bolt an accessory onto it. Which copies have one is the structure's answer — nothing else on a card is somewhere to fit one — so this asks whether the act is there and never what kind of thing the row is. Every control is a link to a real address: the dialog is a server state, so the press that opens it is a navigation and not a script. That also keeps this out of the listing's own form, which wraps every row on the page — a form inside a form is not a thing HTML has. :copies — a list of n26.core.listing.OwnedCopyRow. {% endcomment %}
