Picking a colour scheme is something a reader does once and then never again, and the top bar has one row of space that a page's own name and its switcher want every time. The rows are written out rather than left to the kit's own menu variant because that variant is a dropdown, and a dropdown opened from inside a dropdown is two panels fighting over the same outside click. --- three named states, not a flip --- A two-way toggle can say "dark" but cannot say "the same as everything else on this machine", which is the state most readers are already in and the only one that keeps following the machine after the choice. So the third row exists, and each row says which it is instead of the reader having to work out what pressing would do. `menuitemradio` because that is what these are: one of three, inside a menu, with the current one announced as checked. The tick is drawn for the same reason it is drawn in the switcher — as a picture of what aria-checked has already said, not instead of it. The menu is left open on a press. A reader picking a scheme is usually comparing, and closing the panel would mean reopening it to see the other one. storage_key — where the choice is kept.
