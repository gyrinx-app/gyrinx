{% comment %} — a bound on a number, as a slider in a dropdown. {# one thumb: a ceiling #} {# two thumbs: a floor and a ceiling #} The third of the menu family, after (many of a set) and (one of a set). This is the numeric one: a range you narrow. A slider is the wrong shape for a toolbar — it wants width, and a row of them pushes everything else off a phone — but it is the right shape for "how much", because the useful gesture is drag-until-the-list-looks-right rather than type a number. Putting it behind a trigger keeps both: the control gets its width when open, and the bar keeps a button-sized thing that reads at a glance. Which is what the trigger is for. It states the bound rather than the label alone — "Cost ≤ 80¢", or "Cost 20–80¢" with two thumbs — so a filter bar can be read without opening anything. at_max_label replaces that with a word when the slider excludes nothing, because a bound that filters nothing should not look like a filter. No OK or Cancel, unlike filter-menu. The value applies as it moves: you are watching the list respond, so a confirm step would ask you to approve something you have already seen happen. Nothing to snapshot, either. label — trigger text. model — one Alpine expression, for the one-thumb form: a ceiling. model_min, model_max — two expressions, for the two-thumb form. Setting both switches this to two thumbs; `model` is then ignored. min, max, step — the slider's range. prefix, suffix — wrap the value in the trigger. "≤ " and "¢" give "≤ 80¢". Two thumbs read "20–80¢", so a prefix is usually left off there. at_min_label, at_max_label — words to show instead of the number at each end, for the cases where the number does not say what it means ("Any", not "≤ 200¢"; "Common only", not "≤ R0"). size — the trigger's button size. Default sm, a notch under the kit's; a bar sitting under a long list rather than under a search field wants xs. align, position, min_width — passed through to the dropdown. {% endcomment %}
